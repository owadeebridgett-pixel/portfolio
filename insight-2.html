Understanding the Real Job of an AI Agent

7 min read

🤖

There is a lot of noise around AI, automation and agents right now. Every week, a new tool, model or workflow is marketed as if it will completely transform how business works. But beneath all the hype many people are still unclear on what these things actually do, where they add value and where they do not.

That confusion matters, because not every process needs AI. Sometimes the best solution is simply a basic workflow. If the task is moving data from one point to another, validating a field, or triggering a standard action — then a rule-based automation is often enough. Adding AI in those cases can introduce unnecessary complexity, cost and unpredictability.

This is where the conversation around agents often becomes messy. People use the word “agent” as if it automatically means something advanced, autonomous and intelligent. But an agent is really just a system that can carry out a task on your behalf. That task may be simple or complex. It may involve fixed rules or it may involve reasoning. The intelligence is not what defines the agent — the function does.

AI becomes valuable when the work is not purely mechanical. If the task requires interpretation, pattern recognition, summarisation, categorisation or decision support — then AI can become a powerful layer in the process. But even then, AI should be seen as support, not replacement. The final judgment should still sit with the person responsible for the outcome.

Good automation is not about making everything “smart.” It is about removing friction, reducing wasted effort and making work more efficient.

In practice, this means asking a better question before choosing a tool: What is the actual job to be done? If the job is repetitive and rule-based, automation may be enough. If the job involves ambiguity, judgment or analysis, AI may be worth adding. And if the job is strategic, relational or highly nuanced, human input remains essential.

The strongest systems will not be the most complex ones, but the most appropriate ones. That is the real job of an AI agent: to understand the task, assist where it can and act where it should — without getting in the way of the work that actually matters.
