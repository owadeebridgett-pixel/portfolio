South Africa's Standing with AI & Automation

5 min read

🇿🇦

As I experienced a bit of the workplace, I came to a daunting realisation: the way we work is changing. We may laugh about it, but there is a clear shift happening as more businesses begin to see AI as an asset rather than just another monthly subscription.

I have seen articles about Africa creating incredible and witty automation and AI inventions, and that is what makes this so interesting. AI is being taken seriously and used to its maximum in huge projects that are actively changing people's lives, yet in everyday business operations, it is often used so ineffectively. Huge projects allow room for big innovation, so AI is introduced more easily. But in businesses and firms, very few people really know how to take advantage of this age properly.

The reason is simple: it takes time to learn, test, and figure things out. For many people, that feels like extra work rather than work eliminated. And that is why more roles around AI, automation, and implementation consulting are becoming valuable, slowly but surely.

At the end of the day, operational work and implementation work should never be mixed. I know this from experience. It made me realise why people often prefer what is already known, even when there are better ways to do things. It takes time to learn something new, and not everyone is willing to go through that process immediately.

My time in the workplace has been one of many valuable lessons about where I see myself, or at least what I see myself doing. That is being an implementer — and not just an implementer, but someone who helps companies adopt tools more smoothly. Because the cool thing about automation is that no new system needs to be forced into an existing one. It simply connects the different systems already within the company together.
