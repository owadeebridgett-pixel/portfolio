AI & Work

Who Is Shaping AI?

6 min read

💡

AI is reshaping the world of business, but an equally important question is often left unanswered: who is shaping AI? If AI is changing how work gets done, then the people who design the prompts, set the standards, decide the constraints and review the outputs are shaping the direction of work itself.

The world of work is already changing. AI is helping people communicate faster, analyse information more efficiently and complete administrative tasks that once consumed hours. What used to be a purely manual process is now becoming a human-plus-machine process, where value is not only created by doing the work but by directing the tool well enough to produce a useful result.

That shift is personal for many people entering the workforce today. Coming out of accounting, it was easy to assume the value would come from recording transactions, classifying accounts, and performing reconciliations. Those tasks still matter, but AI has changed how they are done. The real challenge is no longer just completing the task — it is getting AI to complete it well, quickly and in a way that meets the required standard.

That is where the real skill lies.

Using AI effectively is not about asking it to “do the work.” It is about defining what good looks like before the task begins, then checking whether the output meets that standard at the end. The process becomes less about blind automation and more about controlled delegation.

This matters because AI is only valuable when it saves time without destroying quality. If the output is fast but inaccurate, inconsistent, or impossible to trust, then the tool has not added value. The goal is not speed alone. The goal is better work, faster.

This is why the most valuable employees will not necessarily be the ones who use the most AI, but the ones who use it most effectively. They will know how to frame the task, provide the right context, evaluate the result and improve the system over time. The competitive advantage is no longer just technical knowledge — it is judgment, process design and the ability to turn AI into repeatable value.

So when people ask whether AI will replace jobs, the better question may be this: who will be best at shaping AI into something useful? Because the future of work will not belong only to those who can use AI. It will belong to those who can teach it what good work looks like.
